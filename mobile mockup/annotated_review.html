Personalization · Design Review

Why each screen works the way it does

Review of three personalization screens. OPEN items need follow-up tickets; other notes describe implemented behavior.

1. Personalization home
Try the New user toggle, the nudge card, and the kudos card
Tone & framing
1
Thread motif, not a feed
A connecting line presents cards as one view of the user's day, not unrelated notifications.
2
Briefing leads with patterns, not scores
"4 of 6 rings" and "third day this week" provide context without grading the user.
3
Trend card pairs the score with context
The percentage is visible by default, while the Tuesday offsite explains the dip. Users can hide scores in settings; see #29.
4
Missed-task reasons are neutral
"Pushed 3 times" and "no time block set" describe task history, not personal failure.
Transparency & reversibility
5
"Why am I seeing this" explains the reasoning inline
The expanded state shows the basis for the insight, such as "based on 14 days," without sending users elsewhere.
6
"Not now" and "Move it" have equal weight and support undo
Both actions confirm the result and offer a short undo window.
7
Kudos supports reversible Send and Skip actions
The card focuses on one person and moment, with Skip and undo for both choices.
8
Nudge cards show queue position
"1 of 2 today" makes additional nudges visible instead of silently hiding them.
Accessibility & clarity
9
Color is never the only signal
Markers combine color, shape, and glyph: ✦ briefing, ! nudge, ~ trend, · missed, and ♥ kudos. Dark violet text meets AA contrast on light backgrounds.
10
Insight and task chips are visually distinct
Observations such as "Mornings work for you" differ from actions such as "Gym ring open" through color, icon, tap target, and caption. Resolved: the task chip now opens an explainer modal instead of closing a ring inline. Completing a ring may require duration, reps, or streak data; the intended action is a deep link to that ring in the Rings tab.
11
Modal documents deferred product flows
This modal is a mockup placeholder, not a shipping surface. It is shared by the Gym ring chip and New user "Add friends," with content adjusted by entry point. Both actions require multi-step flows, so the modal explains the intended destination rather than faking completion. Users can dismiss it with "Got it" or by tapping outside.
12
New users see explanations, not empty or fabricated cards
The New user state explains why data is unavailable and what will unlock each card.
Every card resolves to an action
13
Trend and missed-task cards provide actions
"This week's pattern" expands to four weekly averages instead of seven daily bars. "What got missed" provides reason-specific actions: "Move to today" for repeatedly delayed tasks and "Add a time block" for unscheduled tasks. Both use confirmation and undo.
Open
14
Sensitive task names may appear on shared screensOPEN
Task names such as "Submit expense report" may be inappropriate on lock-screen previews or screen shares. Add a setting to blur sensitive task names.
15
Friend activity requires friend-side consentOPEN
Showing Jordan's streak in Maya's kudos card requires Jordan's opt-in, not only Maya's settings. This screen cannot meaningfully model that consent flow.
2. Personalization onboarding
Opens on a short expectations screen, then a single-step wizard — Back / Continue / Skip move between steps, with state preserved
Before you start
16
Welcome screen sets expectations before questions
Sample briefing and nudge cards define personalization. Copy explains timing—briefing tonight and nudges in about a week—and confirms results are private. The top bar contains only Back and progress. "Skip setup — use defaults" goes directly to confirmation.
17
Progress begins empty on the welcome screen
The existing four-step bar remains unfilled until question one. Adding a fifth segment would conflict with the "four quick questions" framing and require relabeling every step.
Tone & framing
18
Every step offers a reversible skip
"Skip for now" clarifies that personalization can be configured later.
19
Planning style has no right answer
The subhead explicitly states that no option is correct, encouraging honest choices.
20
Frequency uses a slider; tone uses examples
Frequency and tone remain separate settings. Tone cards show the same nudge in three voices instead of using an abstract Quiet–Cheerful scale. Frequency retains a draggable, keyboard-accessible slider.
21
Nudges are suggestions with a daily limit
The flow states that nudges are not alerts and points to a configurable daily limit.
Trust through the flow
22
Autosave reduces lost-progress concerns
"Your answers save automatically" confirms progress is retained. Per-step time estimates were removed because the welcome screen already sets expectations.
23
Data-use details and confirmation are actionable
"Learn more" expands inline. The confirmation screen shows the user's actual selections and links each item back to its source step.
24
No selected focus times has a clear fallback
"No pattern yet — we'll use your activity instead" explains the zero-selection state.
Open
25
Nudge timing does not account for calendar conflictsOPEN
Historical focus times alone may schedule a nudge during a meeting. Check calendar availability before suggesting specific times.
3. Personalization settings
Every row is interactive — try "Pause personalization," now sitting above "Turn off"
Trust in your personalization
26
"From you" and "Inferred" are distinct
Tags separate explicit user input from observed patterns. User corrections override inferred values.
27
Data-use link opens an explanation
It distinguishes self-reported and inferred data and states that data remains in the account unless the user selects Export.
Fatigue & scoring
28
"Auto-reduce when ignored" is visible and functional
Users can verify that fatigue protection is enabled rather than trusting hidden backend behavior.
29
Users can hide score displays
Scores are on by default. Turning them off changes the trend card to a score-free weekly pattern view.
Reversibility & commitment levels
30
Four distinct commitment levels
"Need a break" pauses nudges only. "Pause personalization" stops all personalization temporarily and resumes automatically. "Turn off" stops it indefinitely. "Delete" is permanent.
31
Paused and destructive states explain their effects
Pause, turn-off, and delete require confirmation that states what is kept or lost. Result banners reflect the action: pause and turn-off offer resume controls and dim inactive sections; delete confirms that patterns and history are gone while personalization remains active for a fresh start.
Accessibility & control
32
Toggle state does not rely only on color
Both knob position and background fill indicate on or off state.
33
Light, Dark, and Auto change the active theme
Theme selection re-renders the screen and supports light sensitivity, migraines, and personal preference.
34
Friend sharing defaults off; kudos suggestions default on
Progress sharing and kudos suggestions are separate controls with different defaults based on sensitivity.
Data rights
35
Export appears before destructive options
Users can retain a copy of their data before choosing to delete it.
Open
36
Personalization still lacks signal-level explainability
Partially resolved: "See what's changed" lists timestamped changes to planning style, tone, and focus times. Changes made on this screen add a live "Just now" entry. The view does not explain the underlying signals for inferred traits, such as specific days or tasks; that requires a separate explainability feature.

Considered, not yet designed

These issues require work beyond screen-level changes and should be tracked in the product specification.

  • Loading and network-error states for all three screens
  • OS-level push notification permission flow before nudges can send
  • Priority and ordering when multiple nudges qualify on one day
  • Calendar-conflict checks before suggesting a specific time
  • Blurred or hidden sensitive task names for lock screens and screen shares
  • Friend-side consent before showing streaks in another user's kudos card
  • Custom kudos messages instead of one canned message
  • Cross-device sync behavior: account-level or device-level
  • Long translated strings affecting chip and button layout
  • RTL mirroring for the thread motif and card alignment
  • Age-appropriate defaults if the product supports minors
  • A full destination screen for "See exactly what data this uses"

Guardrails these decisions map back to

Use these principles to evaluate future personalization features.

Tone & framing

  • Describe, don't judge — State patterns and reasons without judging the user.
  • Scores are optional — Show scores by default, with an option to display only trend shape.

Reversibility

  • Undo low-risk actions — Especially nudge and kudos actions.
  • Confirm high-risk actions — Destructive actions require a confirmation sheet.
  • Use graduated commitment — Pause, turn off, and delete are distinct choices.

Accessibility

  • Never use color alone — Pair color with shape, glyph, or position.
  • Meet AA contrast — Provide usable touch targets and working theme controls.

Honesty about state

  • Design cold starts — Explain unavailable data to new users.
  • Show queues and effects — Surface nudge counts, paused states, and export actions.
  • Make transparency links useful — Links must lead to real explanations.
