Personalization · Design Alternative 2 of 3 · Deck

Why the deck works the way it does

Review of the swipeable card-stack alternative for the same five personalization cards. Only the interaction shell changes; onboarding and settings remain unchanged. OPEN marks known gaps. NEW marks decisions introduced by the deck.

Gained

  • One recommendation per card
  • Swipe to keep, dismiss, or move on
  • Each card has a clear focal point
  • Progress shows where you are in the deck

Traded away

  • Less information visible at once
  • Harder to compare recommendations
  • Easy to skip details by swiping too quickly
  • Swipe gestures are less explicit than buttons
Personalization deck
Drag a card left or right, or use the buttons. Try the "New user" toggle, the ⟲ revisit pill after deferring a card, and the arrow keys.
Tone & framing
1
Swipe stamps and buttons stay violet / sage — never red / green
Avoid reject/accept color coding. Cards retain thread colors: violet for system suggestions, sage for social content, and gray for "not now." No action is framed as wrong.
2
Missed-task and dismissal copy is unchanged from the thread
"Pushed 3 times," "no time block set," and "won't ask again this week" remain neutral and descriptive; only the card layout changes.
3
Briefing and trend never get forced into an accept/reject shape
Either swipe advances read-only cards, and both stamps say "Next." The left/right mechanic is not applied where no decision exists.
Every card resolves to a decision
4
Missed tasks split into two separate cardsNEW
The thread grouped both missed tasks with separate CTAs. The deck resolves one item per swipe, so each task gets its own card and action: "Move to today" or "Add a time block."
5
Decision cards needed a real negative action, so one was inventedNEW
Thread items only offered a positive action. The deck requires both directions, so left swipe means "Leave for now." This is a new decision introduced by the format.
6
Local counters ("1 of 2 today," "1 of 2") stay separate from the deck's own count
Nudge and missed-task counters describe their own queues. They are distinct from the deck progress count, such as "3 of 6 today."
Reversibility & the review list
7
Undo is a toast, not an inline row, and it doesn't expire on a timer
Resolved cards leave the stack, so inline undo is replaced by a toast. It remains until the user taps Undo or resolves the next card.
8
A same-session "to revisit" sheetNEW
"Not now," "Leave for now," and "Skip" add items to the ⟲ revisit pill beside the progress dots. The sheet provides a "Reconsider" action for each item.
9
Reconsidering re-queues a card instead of losing it
"Reconsider" removes an item from the revisit list and returns it to the front of the stack as the next card.
10
Only the most recent action is undo-ableOPEN
The toast supports only the latest action. After a second resolution, the first undo is unavailable. Deferred items remain reachable through revisit; right-swipe actions do not. A multi-level undo stack needs separate design work.
Accessibility & reliability
11
The buttons under the stack are the primary path, not a fallback
Every card has buttons that perform the same actions as swipes. No action requires dragging.
12
Real <button> elements throughout, not div + tabindex + role shim
All interactive controls are native buttons, including pills, actions, why-links, and reconsider rows. Browser-provided focus, Enter/Space support, and screen-reader semantics are retained.
13
Reduced-motion turns physics into a snap
A prefers-reduced-motion query reduces spring, fly-off, and cascade transitions to effectively zero duration without changing behavior.
14
Arrow keys are a keyboard-native swipeNEW
Left and Right arrow keys resolve the top card in the matching direction, equivalent to dragging or tapping.
15
Tapping "why am I seeing this" doesn't get swallowed by the drag gesture
A movement threshold distinguishes taps from drags, preventing inline controls from accidentally triggering swipes.
16
Peeking cards keep their glyph, not just their color
Cards behind the top card retain thread shape-and-glyph coding: ✦ briefing, ! nudge, ~ trend, · missed, and ♥ kudos.
Honesty about state
17
Cold start is one explanatory card, not five empty onesNEW
New users see one card explaining missing content and linking to "Add friends," instead of swiping through five empty cards.
18
"You're all caught up" links somewhere real
The end card links to Tasks and Rings using the existing "this would open X" pattern, and shows any remaining revisit items.
19
Dots and a counter always show where you are
Six dots show resolved, current, and upcoming cards, alongside a count such as "3 of 6 today."
Open
20
Revisit items don't persist past this sessionOPEN
Revisit items are lost when the app closes. Whether they should appear in tomorrow's deck is unresolved.
21
Card order is fixedOPEN
Order is always briefing, nudge, trend, missed ×2, kudos. Priority logic does not yet handle multiple qualifying cards, such as two nudges.
22
Swipe direction isn't mirrored for RTLOPEN
Right = positive and left = negative assumes a left-to-right layout. RTL behavior needs deliberate interaction design, not automatic flipping.
23
Gesture-first has a real cost even with every safety net in placeOPEN
Buttons, arrow keys, and reduced motion help, but drag-to-resolve remains more physically demanding than scrolling and tapping.
24
Missed task names are visible on a shared or unlocked screenOPEN
Task names remain exposed, and may be more prominent because a single card fills the screen.
25
Jordan's streak is shown without Jordan's consentOPEN
Showing Jordan's streak requires Jordan's opt-in, not only Maya's settings. This issue is unchanged from the thread model.

Considered, not yet designed

These issues require work beyond this screen and should be tracked in the specification.

  • Loading and network-error states for the deck
  • Placement of a second same-day nudge: seventh card or tomorrow
  • Priority logic when too many cards qualify
  • Whether revisit items carry into tomorrow or reset at midnight
  • Cross-device sync of in-session revisit state
  • Calendar-conflict checks before suggesting a nudge time
  • Hiding sensitive task names on lock screens or screen shares
  • Friend consent for showing streaks in another person's kudos card
  • Customizable kudos messages
  • i18n support for longer labels and swipe stamps
  • Full RTL layout and swipe-direction mirroring
  • Age-appropriate defaults if the product supports minors

Guardrails these decisions map back to

These principles carry over from the thread model because the feature is unchanged; only the interaction shell differs.

Tone & framing

  • Describe, don't judge — neutral copy across all cards
  • No accept/reject color coding — violet and sage identify the source, not yes or no

Reversibility

  • Undo where it's cheap — a non-expiring toast for the latest action
  • A path back for harder reversals — a same-session revisit sheet

Accessibility

  • Gesture is never the only path — buttons and arrow keys match drag actions
  • Motion is opt-out-able, and native controls provide semantics

Honesty about state

  • Cold start is designed, not blank — one explanatory card replaces empty cards
  • Position is always visible — dots, counter, and an end state
  • Nothing dead-ends — the caught-up card and ring chip link onward
