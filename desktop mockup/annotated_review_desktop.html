Personalization · Desktop Design Review

Why each screen works the way it does

Desktop review of three personalization screens using the same principles as mobile. Mobile open items remain unresolved. DESKTOP notes describe desktop-specific changes; OPEN items need follow-up tickets.

🖥️

About this pass: Tokens were checked against six dark-mode and three light-mode production samples. Accent violet (#854dff) matches production. Dark and light use separate token sets: dark has violet-tinted neutrals; light uses neutral grays. The shared desktop shell—sidebar, top strip, cards, and floating rings widget—is reused across all three files.

1. Personalization home — desktop "For You"
Try the New user toggle (top right), the nudge card, the kudos card, and the sidebar theme switch
Tone & framing
1
Thread motif, not a feed
A visible line connects cards as one view of the user's day, not competing notifications.
2
Briefing leads with pattern, not score
"4 of 6 rings" and "third day this week" provide context without grading the user.
3
Trend card shows the number, with context underneath
The percentage is visible by default; the Tuesday dip is explained as an offsite. Users can hide scores in settings—see #29.
4
Missed-task reasons are descriptive, not judgmental
Reasons such as "Pushed 3 times" and "no time block set" describe task state, not personal failure.
Transparency & reversibility
5
"Why am I seeing this" expands inline
Shows the actual reasoning, such as "based on 14 days...," in place.
6
"Not now" carries equal weight to "Move it," and both are undoable
Dismiss and move are equal actions. Both confirm the result and offer a brief Undo.
7
Kudos works the same way: Send or Skip, either reversible
Kudos offers Send or Skip, with Undo for either choice.
8
Nudge card shows its place in a queue ("1 of 2 today")
Shows that additional nudges exist instead of silently hiding them.
Accessibility & clarity
9
Color is never the only signal
Markers combine color, shape, and glyph (✦ briefing, ! nudge, ~ trend, · missed, ♥ kudos). Dark violet text meets AA contrast on light backgrounds.
10
An "insight" chip and an "open task" chip used to look identical
Insight and open-task chips now use distinct styling. The ring chip has its own color, icon, click target, and caption. Clicking it opens an explainer modal for the real Rings-tab deep link.
11
A modal for a design decision, not a real product surface
This dialog documents deferred interactions, not shipping UI. The Gym ring chip and New user "Add friends" share the modal shell with context-specific content. Dismiss with "Got it" or outside click.
12
Day-one users get an explanation, not a blank or fabricated screen
The New user state explains empty cards and what unlocks them without inventing patterns.
Every card resolves to an action
13
Trend and missed-task cards now do something, not just report something
"See full month" switches from seven daily bars to four weekly averages. Missed tasks provide reason-specific actions: "Move to today" or "Add a time block," each with confirm/undo.

Every row is interactive — try "Pause personalization," the Frequency pills, and the destructive block at the bottom
Trust in your personalization
26
"From you" vs. "Inferred" tags, and corrections always win
Tags distinguish stated preferences from inferred traits. User corrections override inferred values.
27
"See exactly what data this uses" is a real, working link
Explains self-reported versus inferred data and confirms data stays in the account unless the user selects Export.
Fatigue & scoring
28
"Auto-reduce when ignored" is a visible, working toggle
Users can see and control the fatigue-protection setting.
29
Score display can be turned off
Scores show by default. Turning them off changes the trend card to a number-free weekly shape.
Reversibility & commitment levels
30
Four distinct levels: Need a break, Pause personalization, Turn off, Delete
Need a break pauses nudges only. Pause stops personalization temporarily. Turn off is indefinite. Delete is permanent.
31
Destructive or paused states always confirm and show their effect
Pause, turn-off, and delete dialogs explain retained and removed data. Result banners reflect the action; paused sections dim and offer resume actions. Delete has its own non-resumable message.
Accessibility & control

Both knob position and background fill indicate on/off state.
33
Light / Dark / System is a real, working theme swap
Theme selection re-themes the interface, supporting light sensitivity, migraines, and preference.
34
Friend sharing defaults to off, kudos suggestions default to on
Progress sharing and kudos suggestions are separate controls with different defaults.
Data rights
35
Export sits above the destructive options
Users can export their data before choosing destructive actions.

Considered, not yet designed

These issues need broader work than a screen-level fix. They remain unresolved from the mobile review.

  • Loading and network-error states for all three screens
  • Push notification permission flow (OS-level, before any nudge can fire)
  • Priority/ordering logic when multiple nudges qualify the same day
  • Calendar-conflict checking before suggesting a specific time
  • Blurring or hiding sensitive task names on a shared display or screen-share
  • Friend-side consent for surfacing their streaks in someone else's kudos card
  • Customizable kudos message instead of a single canned send
  • Cross-device sync messaging (is this account-level or per-device?)
  • Longer translated strings breaking chip/button layout (i18n)
  • RTL layout mirroring for the thread motif, card alignment, and the sidebar itself
  • Age-appropriate defaults if the product is ever opened to minors
  • An actual screen behind "See exactly what data this uses," not just a dialog

Token reconciliation, in brief

Violet (#854dff) is the existing production accent. Dark and light use separate token sets: dark uses violet/blue-tinted neutrals (#0c0c1a page, #171626 sidebar/card); light uses neutral values (#ffffff page, #f5f5f5 sidebar, #919090 muted text). Sage (#4f8a4b) remains the calmer mockup green for neutral descriptions. Danger red (#ff5c5f) matches production for irreversible actions; the original browner coral remains for non-destructive warnings.

Guardrails these decisions map back to

Desktop applies the same principles as mobile within a different shell.

Tone & framing

  • Describe, don't judge — state patterns and reasons without judging the person
  • Score is optional, not mandatory — default score display can become a number-free trend

Reversibility

  • Undo where it's cheap — especially nudge and kudos actions
  • Confirm where it's not — destructive actions require a dialog
  • Graduated commitment — pause, turn off, and delete are distinct choices

Accessibility

  • Never color-only — shape, glyph, or position also communicates state
  • AA contrast minimum, usable click targets, working themes, and desktop hover states

Honesty about state

  • Cold start is designed, not blank — explain day-one states
  • Queues and effects are visible — show counts, pause banners, and Export
  • Transparency links resolve — no dead-end links
