Personalization · Desktop Design Review

Why each screen works the way it does

The desktop pass of the three personalization screens, paired with the same reasoning-first review as the mobile set. Every original note carries forward unchanged — desktop doesn't resolve any open mobile items — with a new DESKTOP category added to each section for what changed in the conversion itself. Items tagged OPEN are still known gaps worth a ticket.

🖥️

About this pass: tokens were reconciled against six dark-mode and three light-mode samples off the live desktop app rather than eyeballed — the accent violet (#854dff) turned out to already be production's own color, and the two themes are independently-specified value sets (dark carries a real violet tint in its darks; light is neutral gray) rather than one lightness-inverted formula. The shared shell — sidebar, top strip, card language, and the floating rings widget — is built once from the live screenshots and reused across all three files below, the desktop equivalent of the mobile files' repeated .phone/.notch chrome.

1. Personalization home — desktop "For You"
Try the New user toggle (top right), the nudge card, the kudos card, and the sidebar theme switch
Tone & framing
1
Thread motif, not a feed
Cards are connected by a visible line rather than stacked as a generic feed — signals these are one continuous read on the user's day, not unrelated notifications competing for attention.
2
Briefing leads with pattern, not score
"4 of 6 rings" and "third day this week" describe behavior in context. No grade, no implied judgment.
3
Trend card shows the number, with context underneath
The percentage is visible by default, and the note gives the Tuesday dip context (offsite) rather than leaving it to read as a failure. Anyone who'd rather not see a number at all can turn it off in settings — see #29.
4
Missed-task reasons are descriptive, not judgmental
"Pushed 3 times," "no time block set" — observations about the task, never framed as something wrong with the person.
Transparency & reversibility
5
"Why am I seeing this" expands inline
Tapping it shows the actual reasoning ("based on 14 days...") right there, rather than being a transparency gesture with nowhere to go.
6
"Not now" carries equal weight to "Move it," and both are undoable
Dismissal is a first-class action, not a smaller afterthought — and a mis-tap on either button isn't permanent. Both confirm what happened and offer Undo for a few seconds.
7
Kudos works the same way: Send or Skip, either reversible
One friend, one moment — a real "Skip," not a leaderboard, and Undo on both actions so a fast tap doesn't lock in a choice you didn't mean to make.
8
Nudge card shows its place in a queue ("1 of 2 today")
Sets honest expectations that more exist, rather than the home screen silently picking one and hiding the rest.
Accessibility & clarity
9
Color is never the only signal
Each node marker pairs its color with a distinct shape and glyph (✦ briefing, ! nudge, ~ trend, · missed, ♥ kudos), and text uses a darker violet that clears AA contrast against light backgrounds.
10
An "insight" chip and an "open task" chip used to look identical
"Mornings work for you" (an observation) and "Gym ring open" (an action) have distinct styling — the ring chip has its own color, icon, and a real click target — plus a caption spelling out the difference. Clicking it opens a short explainer modal rather than faking a completion the mockup can't actually represent, stating plainly that the real interaction is a deep link into the Rings tab scoped to that ring.
11
A modal for a design decision, not a real product surface
This confirm-style dialog isn't meant to model UI that ships — it's a placeholder documenting an intentional deferral. Shared by two entry points: the Gym ring chip and "Add friends" in the New user state's kudos card both open the same modal shell, icon/title/body swapped in for which one was clicked. Dismissible by "Got it" or by clicking outside it.
12
Day-one users get an explanation, not a blank or fabricated screen
The New user state explains why each card is empty and what unlocks it, instead of inventing a pattern that doesn't exist yet.
Every card resolves to an action
13
Trend and missed-task cards now do something, not just report something
"This week's pattern" gets a "See full month" toggle that swaps in an actual second data view (4 weekly averages instead of 7 daily bars). "What got missed" gets a per-item action tied to the specific reason it lists — the repeatedly-pushed expense report gets "Move to today," the dentist call gets "Add a time block" — both resolving with the same confirm/undo pattern already used for nudges and kudos.
Open
14
Missed task names are visible on a shared or unlocked screenOPEN
"Submit expense report" sitting on a home screen is fine alone, less fine on a shared display or a screen-share. Worth a "blur sensitive task names" setting.
15
Jordan's streak is shown to someone else without Jordan's consentOPEN
The kudos card surfaces another person's activity. That needs its own opt-in on Jordan's side, not just the viewer's settings. Harder to mock up meaningfully here — this view is Maya's alone, so there's no real surface in this file to grant or preview Jordan's consent from.
Desktop conversion
37
"For You" is a dedicated top-level sidebar itemNEW
Sits between Home and Calendar, same tier as the rest of primary nav — not nested under Home or Feed. The bottom tab bar is gone entirely; the sidebar is the only nav surface on desktop.
38
The trend card moved to a static right railNEW
Freed-up horizontal space goes to a right rail for "This week's pattern," not a second thread column — keeps the thread reading as one continuous column (note #1) at a comfortable ~660px width. The rail card echoes the Profile page's productivity gauge (same label style, same violet stroke weight, a thin decorative arc behind the numeral) without becoming a literal instance of it — it's still the same bar-chart component underneath, just visually rhyming with the gauge so the two clearly belong to one design system.
39
The state switcher moved to the top strip
Sits top-right of the content pane next to the settings gear, matching where the live app already places page-level controls (Calendar's Week/Month toggle occupies the same spot).
40
Deep-link modals recenter as true page-level overlays
No more inset:0 on an artificial phone bezel — the dim layer and centered card now cover the actual app frame, sidebar included, the way a real modal would block the whole app during a placeholder interaction.
41
Hover states added throughout
The mobile CSS never defined :hover — no reason to, mobile has none. Every clickable row, chip, and button gets a subtle background lift or border shift for desktop. Existing :focus-visible outlines and keyboard handling were already solid and carried over unchanged.
2. Personalization onboarding — desktop
Opens on a short expectations screen, then a single-step wizard — Back / Continue / Skip move between steps, with state preserved
Before you start
16
A welcome screen sets expectations before question one
Shows a sample briefing and a sample nudge so "personalization" means something concrete, then a single privacy/timing line covers when to expect results and confirms it's visible only to you. A "Skip setup — use defaults" link jumps straight to confirm for anyone who'd rather not answer anything at all.
17
Progress bar carries over, but stays unfilled here
Reuses the same 4-segment bar from the question steps with nothing filled in yet, rather than adding a 5th segment that would contradict the "four quick questions" framing. The first segment fills the moment you land on question one.
Tone & framing
18
"Skip for now" is on every step, and worded so it's clearly reversible
Personalization is additive, never a gate — "you can set this up later" removes the fear that skipping forfeits it permanently.
19
No right-answer framing on planning style
The subhead states directly that there's no correct option — pre-empts the instinct to answer how Kindred "wants" rather than how someone actually behaves.
20
Frequency stays a slider; tone became sample-cards
"How much" and "how it sounds" are still two separate settings. Tone no longer asks someone to place themselves on an abstract scale: it shows the same nudge written three ways and asks which sounds like them. Frequency keeps its draggable track with keyboard arrow-key support.
21
"Nudges are suggestions, not alerts," with a stated daily ceiling
Sets expectations before a single nudge has been sent, and points to a concrete limit in settings so "light touch" isn't just a vibe.
Trust through the flow
22
An autosave note at the end removes the "did I lose this?" worry
"Your answers save automatically" removes the anxiety of losing progress mid-flow.
23
A real "Learn more" panel, and an editable, live summary before commit
The confirm screen reads back your actual choices from every prior step — not placeholder text — and each item links straight back to where it came from.
24
Deselecting every focus time has an explicit fallback
"No pattern yet — we'll use your activity instead" replaces a silent, unexplained zero-selection state.
Open
25
No calendar-conflict awareness in nudge timingOPEN
Onboarding asks when someone's sharpest, but a later nudge ("Move to 8:40am?") could land on a meeting. Worth checking calendar availability, not just historical pattern.
Desktop conversion
42
A centered modal replaces the full-screen mobile wizardNEW
None of the screenshots showed an existing modal/wizard pattern to match, so this was genuinely greenfield. Stretching the mobile screen edge-to-edge would read as an unstyled mobile page — instead it's a 600px panel with the same card radius/border/shadow language as everything else, sitting on top of the dimmed sidebar+content shell rather than replacing it.
43
Step 0's two preview cards sit side by sideNEW
A genuine improvement the extra width unlocks — a 392px phone had to stack "Example · Evening briefing" and "Example · Suggested nudge"; a 600px modal has the room to show both at once.
44
The modal intentionally blocks the sidebar underneath
The dimmed backdrop covers the full app shell, including the theme toggle — standard modal-trap behavior, matching how a real dialog holds focus until it's dismissed. Verified by testing: the sidebar simply isn't clickable while onboarding is open.
45
Slider drag needed no rework
setupSlider() is built on pointerdown/pointermove/pointerup with a touch fallback — pointer events already cover mouse interaction natively, confirmed by drag-testing the frequency track with a real pointer sequence.
3. Personalization settings — desktop
Every row is interactive — try "Pause personalization," the Frequency pills, and the destructive block at the bottom
Trust in your personalization
26
"From you" vs. "Inferred" tags, and corrections always win
Distinguishes what the user explicitly stated from what personalization observed — and states plainly that a correction overrides what's inferred, so fixing it feels like correcting a guess, not arguing with the system.
27
"See exactly what data this uses" is a real, working link
Opens an explanation of what's self-reported vs. inferred and confirms nothing leaves the account unless the person clicks Export.
Fatigue & scoring
28
"Auto-reduce when ignored" is a visible, working toggle
The fatigue-protection guardrail isn't just backend behavior — it's something the user can see is on, building confidence it won't nag.
29
Score display can be turned off
The percentage shows by default. Turning this off swaps the trend card to a "no single score" framing — just the shape of the week, with no number — for anyone who finds a percentage stressful rather than motivating.
Reversibility & commitment levels
30
Four distinct levels: Need a break, Pause personalization, Turn off, Delete
"Need a break" pauses nudges only. "Pause personalization" stops everything for a chosen duration and resumes automatically. "Turn off" is indefinite until manually resumed. "Delete" is the only one that's permanent. Each is a meaningfully different commitment, not variations of one button.
31
Destructive or paused states always confirm and show their effect
Confirming a pause, turn-off, or delete opens a dialog stating exactly what's kept and lost, then shows a banner reflecting what happened — pause and turn-off offer Resume / Turn back on and dim the sections that stop updating; delete shows its own message since resuming isn't really what happened there, and personalization stays active rather than appearing off.
Accessibility & control
32
Toggle state never relies on color alone
The knob's position and the background fill both change, so state reads correctly even for users who can't distinguish the on/off color difference.
33
Light / Dark / System is a real, working theme swap
Relevant for migraine, light sensitivity, and plain preference — selecting Dark actually re-themes the screen rather than just marking a button as selected.
34
Friend sharing defaults to off, kudos suggestions default to on
Low-stakes social (suggesting kudos) and higher-stakes social (sharing your own progress/scores) are split into separate toggles with different defaults.
Data rights
35
Export sits above the destructive options
Someone shouldn't have to choose between keeping a copy of their data and deleting it — exporting is always available first.
Open
36
"Your personalization" still has no deep audit trail
Resolved, partially: a "See what's changed" link lists each change to planning style, tone, and focus times with a timestamp, wired to real interactions — cycling a value or toggling a focus-time chip adds a live "Just now" entry immediately. What this doesn't cover: the specific signals behind an inferred trait (which days, which tasks) — that's a deeper explainability feature, not a change log, and still worth its own ticket.
Desktop conversion
46
Restructured into APPEARANCE / NOTIFICATIONS / PERSONALIZATION / FRIENDS & SHARING / PERSONALIZATION DATANEW
Production has no left-rail settings nav — categorization happens through named sections on one scrolling page. Appearance folds into production's real Theme control rather than duplicating the mockup's own light/dark/auto picker. "Your personalization" (learned traits) and "Reports & briefings" combine under one new PERSONALIZATION eyebrow, since nothing in production covers either today.
47
Destructive rows split into their own red-styled blockNEW
Mirrors production's own pattern of separating safe, reversible controls from irreversible ones. Export, Reset, and Pause personalization keep ordinary row styling in PERSONALIZATION DATA; "Turn off personalization" and "Delete all personalization data" pull into a visually separated red-tinted block below — red label, red trailing chevron, no toggle — matching production's Delete Account / Log Out treatment (sampled at roughly #ff5c5f, a brighter, more saturated red than the mockup's own browner coral, which now reads as the non-destructive warning tone).
48
Frequency rebuilt as a segmented pill controlNEW
Matches production's Check-in Frequency pattern exactly: a full-width rounded track with a lighter neutral pill behind the selected label (not a violet fill — production's own segmented controls use a neutral highlight, confirmed by sampling the Theme row) and plain muted labels for the rest. Replaces the mobile file's click-to-cycle mini-track.
49
Bottom sheets convert to centered confirmation modalsNEW
Every sheet — pause / turn off / delete / reset confirmations, the audit log — keeps identical copy, actions, and danger styling, just swaps the container and entry animation from slide-up to a centered dialog. Bottom sheets are a mobile reachability pattern with no real desktop equivalent.
50
Theme control is wired to one source of truth
The sidebar's sun/moon icon is a true bidirectional quick-toggle between light and dark; this settings page's System/Light/Dark control reads and writes the same underlying state, so switching one updates the other immediately rather than the two drifting out of sync.

Considered, not yet designed

Real problems worth a ticket, but solving them changes scope beyond a screen-level fix — carried forward unchanged from the mobile review, since desktop doesn't resolve any of them.

  • Loading and network-error states for all three screens
  • Push notification permission flow (OS-level, before any nudge can fire)
  • Priority/ordering logic when multiple nudges qualify the same day
  • Calendar-conflict checking before suggesting a specific time
  • Blurring or hiding sensitive task names on a shared display or screen-share
  • Friend-side consent for surfacing their streaks in someone else's kudos card
  • Customizable kudos message instead of a single canned send
  • Cross-device sync messaging (is this account-level or per-device?)
  • Longer translated strings breaking chip/button layout (i18n)
  • RTL layout mirroring for the thread motif, card alignment, and the sidebar itself
  • Age-appropriate defaults if the product is ever opened to minors
  • An actual screen behind "See exactly what data this uses," not just a dialog

Token reconciliation, in brief

Full sampling and reasoning lives in the conversion guide this pass was built from; the short version: violet (#854dff) is unchanged everywhere — it's already production's shipped accent, not a new choice. Dark and light are independently-specified rather than one lightness-inverted formula, since dark's darks (#0c0c1a page, #171626 sidebar/card) carry a real violet/blue tint while light's neutrals (#ffffff page, #f5f5f5 sidebar, #919090 muted text) are genuinely tint-free. Sage stayed the mockup's own calmer #4f8a4b rather than production's brighter status-dot green, matching the "describe, don't judge" tone the review argues for throughout. Danger red now follows production's brighter #ff5c5f for genuinely irreversible actions, while the mockup's original browner coral still covers softer, non-destructive warnings.

Guardrails these decisions map back to

The recurring principles behind the choices above, unchanged from the mobile review — the desktop pass follows the same guardrails, it just has a different shell to apply them in.

Tone & framing

  • Describe, don't judge — patterns and reasons are stated neutrally, never as a verdict on the person
  • Score is optional, not mandatory — shown by default, with a settings toggle to drop to trend shape only, no number

Reversibility

  • Undo where it's cheap — nudge and kudos actions, in particular
  • Confirm where it's not — anything destructive gets a dialog, not a click
  • Graduated commitment — pause, turn off, and delete are not the same decision

Accessibility

  • Never color-only — shape, glyph, or position always carries state too
  • AA contrast minimum, real click targets, working theme control, and now hover states throughout

Honesty about state

  • Cold start is designed, not blank — day-one users get an explanation
  • Queues and effects are visible — "1 of 2," a banner when paused, a real Export
  • Transparency links resolve — no link that dead-ends
